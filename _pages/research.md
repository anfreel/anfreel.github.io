My research examines how people evaluate and respond to artificial intelligence, particularly when AI recommendations challenge their own judgments. Drawing on human–computer interaction, cognitive science, and empirical methods, I study how AI system design influences trust, decision-making, and human autonomy.

## Human Judgment and AI Advice

A central focus of my research is understanding how people evaluate AI-generated recommendations and decide whether to accept, question, or reject them.

My work investigates how features of AI-assisted decision environments, including the timing and severity of errors, influence people's perceptions of AI systems. In related research, I examine how individuals respond to AI advice that conflicts with decisions they have already made.

Together, these studies investigate how interaction design shapes people's judgments and the conditions under which AI assistance supports meaningful human decision-making.

**Selected publication:** [How Misclassification Severity and Timing Influence User Trust in AI Image Classification](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715275.3732187) — ACM FAccT 2025.

## Social Influence and Persuasion in AI

I investigate how social information surrounding AI recommendations influences human decision-making, including how perceived agreement among experts or peers shapes responses to algorithmic advice.

Building on these questions, I am interested in the persuasive capabilities of conversational AI systems and how different forms of communication influence people's judgments. In particular, I am interested in distinguishing persuasion that supports informed reasoning from design practices that may undermine independent judgment.

## AI Governance and Accountability

My interdisciplinary interests extend to the legal and policy challenges of artificial intelligence, particularly questions of accountability and responsibility for AI-related harms.

I am interested in how legal frameworks assign responsibility across the organizations involved in developing, integrating, and deploying AI systems. These questions complement my empirical research by examining how technological design decisions, institutional practices, and legal obligations shape the responsible use of AI.
