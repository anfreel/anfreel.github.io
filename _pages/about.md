## About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Informatics at Indiana University Bloomington, advised by Professor Apu Kapadia. My research examines how people evaluate and respond to artificial intelligence, particularly when AI recommendations conflict with their own judgments. I am interested in understanding when people rely on AI advice, when they question it, and how the design of AI systems influences these decisions.

Drawing on human–computer interaction, cognitive science, and experimental methods, I investigate the behavioral and contextual factors that shape human–AI decision-making. My work examines how features of AI-assisted decision environments, including the timing and presentation of recommendations, influence people's responses to algorithmic advice. More broadly, I seek to understand how AI systems can be designed to support critical evaluation and meaningful human judgment rather than simply encourage reliance.

My research interests also extend to the legal and policy dimensions of artificial intelligence, particularly questions of accountability, responsibility, and AI governance. Alongside my doctoral research, I work in cybersecurity risk management at Northwestern Mutual, where I evaluate technological risks and organizational security practices. My interdisciplinary background in cognitive science, cybersecurity, law, and business informs my approach to studying both the human and institutional implications of emerging technologies.
