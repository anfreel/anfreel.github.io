## About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Informatics at Indiana University Bloomington, advised by Professor Apu Kapadia. My research examines how people evaluate and respond to artificial intelligence, particularly when AI recommendations conflict with their own judgments. I am interested in understanding how the design of AI systems influences trust, reliance, and human decision-making.

Drawing on human–computer interaction, cognitive science, and empirical methods, I investigate how people respond to algorithmic advice and the conditions under which AI systems shape their decisions. My research has examined how the timing and severity of AI errors affect user trust, while my ongoing work explores disagreement, social influence, and the presentation of AI-generated recommendations. More broadly, I am interested in how AI systems can support critical evaluation and meaningful human judgment.

My interdisciplinary interests also extend to AI governance and accountability, particularly the legal and institutional challenges of addressing AI-related harms. Alongside my doctoral research, I work in cybersecurity risk management at Northwestern Mutual, where I assess technological risks and organizational security practices. My background in cognitive science, cybersecurity, and law informs my approach to understanding both the human and institutional implications of emerging technologies.
